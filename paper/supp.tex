\section{Certification: statements and proofs}
\label{supp:proofs}

\paragraphb{Learn-then-Test with fixed-sequence testing.}
Let $\lambda_1\prec\lambda_2\prec\dots\prec\lambda_L$ be the parameters of a policy family, ordered from conservative to aggressive, and let $\hat R_n(\lambda)$ be the empirical risk of the policy on $n$ calibration pairs (the fraction of pairs registered by the reference but not by the policy).
For each $\lambda_l$ we test $H_l: R(\lambda_l)>\varepsilon$ with the exact binomial $p$-value $p_l=\Pr\big(\mathrm{Bin}(n,\varepsilon)\le n\hat R_n(\lambda_l)\big)$, and return $\hat\lambda=\lambda_{l^*}$ with $l^*=\max\{l: p_1,\dots,p_l\le\delta\}$ (no policy if $p_1>\delta$).
Because the order is fixed before seeing the data, testing in sequence controls the family-wise error rate at $\delta$, so $\Pr\big(R(\hat\lambda)\le\varepsilon\big)\ge1-\delta$ when calibration and test pairs are exchangeable~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt}.
With no loss among the $n$ calibration pairs, $p_1=(1-\varepsilon)^n$, so no level-$\varepsilon$ certificate exists unless $n\ge\log\delta/\log(1-\varepsilon)$: 230 pairs for $\varepsilon=1$\,\% and $\delta=0.1$, and 339 per group at level $\delta/3$.

\paragraphb{Proof of Proposition~1.}
Let the groups $g=1,\dots,G$ be defined by an observable $g(z_1)$ of the first stage, and let $\hat\lambda_g$ be returned by the procedure above on the calibration pairs of group $g$ at level $\delta/G$.
Define the events $\mathcal E_g=\{R_g(\hat\lambda_g)\le\varepsilon\}$, where $R_g$ is the risk on test pairs of group $g$.
By exchangeability within groups, $\Pr(\mathcal E_g^c)\le\delta/G$, so by the union bound $\Pr\big(\cap_g\mathcal E_g\big)\ge1-\delta$.
On this event, for a test distribution with arbitrary group proportions $w_g\ge0$, $\sum_g w_g=1$, the risk of the combined policy is $R=\sum_g w_g R_g(\hat\lambda_g)\le\varepsilon$. \hfill$\square$

The marginal certificate needs exchangeability of the whole calibration and test distributions, which fails when the test set has a different mix of easy and hard pairs (\eg the KITTI raw drives, whose full-budget recall is \val{sc.kr.compat.group.full}\,\% against \val{sc.ki.compat.group.full}\,\% on the odometry pairs that calibrate them).
The conditional certificate only needs exchangeability within each group.

\paragraphb{Choosing the candidate.}
The statements above hold for one candidate (ladder, policy family and its fixed sequence) and one taxonomy (group edges) that do not depend on the calibration pairs.
We choose both on the fitting pairs only, and use nothing of the calibration set but its size, which fixes how many losses a certificate can accept; conditionally on the fitting pairs, the chosen candidate is fixed and the guarantee applies to it unchanged.
Choosing instead the cheapest of $L$ candidates certified on the same calibration pairs only guarantees $1-L\delta$ by the union bound, and the cheapest is the one whose certificate most likely erred on the aggressive side.

\paragraphb{Truncated ladders.}
If the last budget $N_K$ of a ladder is below the number of correspondences, even the policy that never stops early has a positive risk.
We prepend this always-continue policy to every fixed sequence as $\lambda_0$; the sequence is still fixed in advance, so the guarantee above is unchanged, and a ladder whose $H_0$ is not rejected (in some group, for the conditional certificate) yields no certified policy.

\section{Implementation and protocol}
\label{supp:impl}

\paragraphb{Protocol v2.}
All splits, rules and configurations of the paper's experiments were pre-registered (file \texttt{PROTOCOL.md} of the code release) before the new data were downloaded or processed.
\emph{Indoor}: the 75 3DMatch training scenes are permuted with seed 20261019 and assigned to the blind test until it holds at least 3{,}500 pairs with overlap above 10\,\% (9 scenes), then to calibration until it holds as many (13 scenes); the rest are fitting scenes.
The blind test is a random sample of 3{,}000 of its pairs, reported above and below 30\,\% overlap; calibration is a random sample of \val{v2_indoor_fpfh.n.cal} pairs, and the \val{v2_indoor_fpfh.n.fit} fitting pairs are the earlier training sample restricted to fitting scenes, topped up at random.
The 3DMatch validation scenes, which served the design, are not used with FPFH.
\emph{Outdoor}: see the KITTI paragraph below.
\emph{Deviation}: the raw drives outside odometry sequences 0--10 yield 1{,}332 pairs under FCGF's rule, not the 2{,}400 the outdoor split assumed; before any of their scans was downloaded, the split was amended so that FPFH is certified on odometry pairs and tested on all raw-drive pairs, and FCGF is certified on 42 drives and tested on the other 7.
\looseness=-1 Each blind test set was evaluated once, after every selection had been written to disk.

\paragraphb{Correspondences.}
FPFH~\cite{rusu2009fpfh} on 5\,cm voxels (indoors) or 30\,cm voxels (outdoors), with normal and feature radii of 2 and 5 voxels.
For each source point we take the nearest target descriptor, sort the matches by Lowe ratio and keep at most 4{,}000.

\paragraphb{Estimators.}
\SCP and TurboReg are re-implemented in NumPy following the papers and the official code.
MAC~\cite{zhang2023mac}, the third estimator, added by pre-registered addenda to protocol~v3 before it ran on any test pair, follows the authors' implementations: the first-order compatibility $\max(0,1-d^2/\tau^2)$ with $\tau$ the inlier threshold ($10$\,cm indoors, $60$\,cm on KITTI), pruned below 0.99 (their Python implementation) and, with FCGF on KITTI, below 0.999 (their C++ implementation; its value for FPFH on KITTI, 0.9, registered the same 47 KITTI fitting pairs but found up to 25 million maximal cliques per run, so we keep 0.99 there); the second-order graph; the C++ implementation's graph reduction, which keeps only the edges among the most clustered correspondences (at least about 50, by weighted clustering coefficient and an Otsu threshold) when the graph is dense; maximal cliques of at least three nodes, four on KITTI (igraph~\cite{csardi2006igraph}); node-guided clique selection, one SVD hypothesis per selected clique and the MAE score.
The reduction matters for cost: on an easy indoor fitting pair with many inliers, the clique search without it finds 16.5 million maximal cliques and takes minutes, with it about two thousand and two seconds.
The authors' code uses the ground truth in one place each (the Python demo keeps the refined pose only if it is closer to the ground truth; the C++ code judges success before refining); we always refine and judge the refined pose, as for the other estimators.
\Cref{tab:reimpl} compares their full-budget recall with the published numbers.
With FCGF features, the input closest to the published runs, all three come within \val{fid.fcgf.indoor.gap.min}--\val{fid.fcgf.indoor.gap.max}\pp of the published recall on 3DMatch and 3DLoMatch, so most of the \val{fid.fpfh.indoor.gap.min}--\val{fid.fpfh.indoor.gap.max}\pp gap with FPFH likely lies in our FPFH correspondences rather than in the estimators; on KITTI the gap of \SCP and TurboReg remains \val{fid.fcgf.kitti.gap.min}--\val{fid.fcgf.kitti.gap.max}\pp with FCGF, where their thresholds were chosen on FPFH validation pairs, and that of MAC, with its authors' KITTI settings, is \val{fid.mc.fcgf.ki.gap}\pp.
Every speed-up in the paper compares an estimator with itself, so implementation speed cancels out to first order.

\paragraphb{KITTI odometry (development).}
We follow the FCGF/Predator/GeoTransformer protocol~\cite{choy2019fcgf,huang2021predator,qin2022geotransformer} and use GeoTransformer's pairs and ICP-refined ground truth: sequences 0--5 for training, 6--7 for validation and 8--10 for testing (\val{ki.n.test} test pairs).
The official velodyne archive (85\,GB) supports HTTP range requests, so we read its index remotely and download only the scans used by these pairs, voxelised at 0.3\,m; \val{ki.n.train} training pairs fit the controller, and the other \val{ki.n.calib} training pairs and the \val{ki.n.val} validation pairs certify it.
A pair is registered if RE${}\le5^\circ$ and TE${}\le60$\,cm.
Nearest descriptors are found by brute force outdoors (a $k$-d tree degrades on its many near-identical ground descriptors, taking minutes per pair); on 3DMatch pairs the two searches agree on 99.9\,\% of the nearest neighbours.
Distance thresholds scale with the inlier threshold (0.6\,m vs.\ 0.1\,m indoors); the compatibility thresholds of the two estimators were chosen by full-budget recall on the validation sequences (\cref{tab:kittithr}), and the ordering uses \SCP's threshold.

\paragraphb{KITTI raw drives (blind).}
The KITTI raw recordings contain 145 drives that belong to none of the odometry sequences 0--10, so neither FCGF nor this work had used them; 49 of them move far enough to form pairs.
Scans are fetched by HTTP range requests from the official archives.
Pairs follow FCGF's rule: start at the first frame, take as partner the frame before the first one more than 10\,m away, and start again after the partner, which gives 1{,}332 pairs.
Ground truth comes from the drive's GPS/IMU poses refined by point-to-point ICP exactly as in FCGF (5\,cm voxels, 0.2\,m correspondence distance, 200 iterations); 22 pairs on which ICP moved the pose by more than 1\,m or $5^\circ$ (a failed refinement, by the pre-registered rule) were dropped, leaving \val{kr.n.test} pairs.
FPFH is tested on all of them; FCGF is certified on the \val{v2_kitti_fcgf.n.cal} pairs of 42 drives and tested on the \val{fkr.n.test} pairs of the other 7, assigned by a seeded permutation of the drives.

\paragraphb{FCGF.}
FCGF~\cite{choy2019fcgf} features exist only for the test sets, so we re-implemented the network (a sparse residual U-Net) in plain PyTorch, with MinkowskiEngine's quantisation and kernel ordering, and load the official weights (3DMatch, 5\,cm; KITTI, 30\,cm, 32 dimensions) with a restricted unpickler (\texttt{acpr.safe\_torch\_load}), so that no code stored in a checkpoint can run.

Run on the input points of the released test features (8 3DMatch fragments and 4 KITTI scans, every keypoint), it reproduces them with a minimum cosine similarity of 1.00000 and an absolute difference of at most $1.2\times10^{-6}$ (\url{results/protocol_v2/fcgf_verification.txt} in the code release), so the FCGF results do not depend on the re-implementation.
With it we compute FCGF features for every split.
FCGF was trained on all 3DMatch training scenes and on odometry sequences 0--5: indoors it is fitted on the \val{v2_indoor_fcgf.n.fit} fitting pairs, certified on the \val{v2_indoor_fcgf.n.cal} pairs of the 3DMatch validation scenes (not used in FCGF's training) and tested on the development benchmarks; outdoors it is fitted on the \val{v2_kitti_fcgf.n.fit} odometry training pairs and certified and tested on the raw drives.
Correspondences, budgets and estimator thresholds are those of the FPFH experiments.
On the odometry training pairs nearly every first stage of FCGF succeeds, so the outdoor controller sees almost no failure; the certificate then rejects every early stop on the calibration drives, and \method runs the full budget (\mainref{sec:shift}{Sec.~4.3}).

\paragraphb{Controller.}
Features (per stage): $\log(1+\text{inliers})$, inlier fraction of the budget, fraction of all correspondences and of the 250 most distinctive explained by the estimate (inlier threshold of the dataset), $\log(1+\text{pose change})$ in degrees and metres and an agreement flag ($<5^\circ$, $<0.2$\,m), the stage index, $\log N$, the median Lowe ratio of the budget, and the gain in explained correspondences.
$p_\text{now}$ and $p_\text{fin}$ are logistic regressions (standardised features, $C=1$) fitted on all stages of the fitting pairs (indoors: the \val{v2_indoor_fpfh.n.fit} fitting pairs; KITTI: \val{ki.n.train} odometry training pairs; FCGF: the same pairs with FCGF features).
Threshold grids: $\lambda\in\{1.01,0.995,\dots,0.5\}$ (18 values), $\gamma\in\{0,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.15,0.2,0.3\}$, and 42 cost prices for the Markov controller.
Difficulty groups are the terciles, on the fitting pairs, of $\log(1+\text{explained correspondences})$ after the first stage.
Candidate ladders: nine that end at the full budget (from 250 or 500, through subsets of 1{,}000 and 2{,}000; among them the doubling and $\times4$ ladders), four that end at 2{,}000, and the single budgets 1{,}000 and 2{,}000 (15 ladders, six of which end below 4{,}000), plus the full budget alone, which needs no certificate.
\emph{Choice} (\mainref{sec:certification}{Sec.~3.4}): the fitting pairs are split into five folds, each scored by a controller fitted on the other four; for every candidate (ladder and family; for give-up also $\gamma$), the most aggressive parameters of its fixed sequence with at most $k$ losses on these pairs are taken, where $k/n$ is the largest loss share the exact binomial test accepts for the calibration set's size $n$ (no loss per group for the group-conditional certificate with our pools, about $0.6$\,\% for the marginal one), and the candidate with the lowest mean time is certified.

\paragraphb{Cost accounting and timing.}
Each distinct computation is charged once: the ordering when the first truncated budget is visited, and a budget that covers all correspondences only once even if several ladder stages coincide.
\emph{Operation counts} are hardware-independent: each run is charged the dominant arithmetic of its steps on its $N$ correspondences.
For \SCP: $N^2$ compatibility tests, $10N^2$ for the power iterations of the leading eigenvector, $SN^2$ for the second-order compatibility of the $S$ seeds, $k_1^3+k_2^3$ per seed for local consensus, $SN$ for scoring the hypotheses and $N$ per refinement iteration.
For TurboReg: $N^2$ tests and $N^3$ for the second-order graph, $K_1N$ for the third vertex of the $K_1$ pivot edges, $N$ per 3-clique hypothesis and per refinement iteration.
For MAC: $N^2$ tests and $N^3$ for the second-order graph, $\sum_i\deg_i^2/2$ for the clustering coefficients of the graph reduction, $k^2$ per enumerated maximal clique of $k$ nodes for its weight, and $N$ per selected clique's hypothesis and per refinement iteration.
For our ordering: $P^2$ tests among the $P=500$ most distinctive correspondences, then $Nm+Nm^2$ for the $m=100$ anchors.
The ratio of the mean count of the full budget to that of a configuration, over the full test set, is the operation reduction of \mainref{tab:blind}{Tables~1 and~2}.
The counts ignore fixed per-run overheads and data-dependent work that leaves the leading terms unchanged, such as TurboReg's fuller clique search on consistent input (\cref{supp:results}), so they complement rather than replace wall-clock time.
Offline costs come from per-pair logs of every stage, timed single-threaded with six parallel workers; each method is compared with a reference timed in the same run.
All wall-clock claims are confirmed live: one process with the default multi-threaded BLAS (OpenBLAS, 16 threads), the same random test pairs for every configuration, and the order of the runs rotated from pair to pair. Offline (single-threaded) and live (multi-threaded) speed-ups can differ, since multi-threading does not accelerate small and large budgets equally: \val{sc.bh.compat.group.speed}\X offline vs.\ \val{sc.bh.compat.live.speed}\X live for \SCP on the held-out scenes, \val{sc.ki.compat.group.speed}\X vs.\ \val{sc.ki.compat.live.speed}\X on KITTI odometry and \val{sc.fm.compat.group.speed}\X vs.\ \val{sc.fm.compat.live.speed}\X on FCGF 3DMatch; the paper therefore reports live speed-ups.
Live and offline stopping decisions agreed on at least \val{live.agree.minall}\,\% of the pairs in every live run and on at least \val{live.agree.indoor.min}\,\% indoors; the differences come from multi-threaded arithmetic, which on KITTI also changes a few nearest neighbours (its brute-force nearest-neighbour search sums in a different order with several threads).
A pair is timed again when one of its runs takes more than ten times the median full-budget time of its test set, which no run can do legitimately (an adaptive run costs at most about two full runs); no pair of the protocol-v2 timing runs met it (\val{live.retimed} re-timed pairs). For MAC, whose cost varies by orders of magnitude between pairs with the number of maximal cliques, the reference is the pair's own logged full-budget time (\val{mc.live.retimed} re-timed pairs).

\section{Protocol v3: the hardness gate and the fresh blind test}
\label{supp:v3}

\paragraphb{Why a second blind test.}
After the protocol-v2 blind evaluation, exploration on fitting, calibration and development data only (the blind sets were never loaded) found two improvements: choosing the controller's classifier on the fitting pairs, and a hardness gate.
Because they were found after the v2 blind sets had been seen, the final method was tested again on fresh data under a second pre-registered protocol (file \texttt{PROTOCOL.md}, committed before the split was generated).

\paragraphb{Data.}
The 53 fitting scenes of protocol~v2 were permuted with seed 20261021 and assigned to the fresh blind test until it held at least 3{,}500 pairs with overlap above 10\,\% (\val{v3.n.scenes} scenes); \val{v3.n.sampled} pairs were sampled at random.
Before any of them was processed, a leakage check found that \val{v3.n.dropped} had appeared in earlier logs as fitting or training pairs (never as test pairs: protocol-v2 fitting pairs, the first protocol's training sample and the pose-search training logs); they were removed (deviation~1 of protocol~v3), leaving \val{v3.n.blind} pairs that no experiment had processed under any role, \val{th.n.test} above and \val{tl.n.test} below 30\,\% overlap.
The final method is fitted on \val{v3.n.fitkept} protocol-v2 fitting pairs outside these scenes and \val{v3.n.fitnew} new pairs of the remaining fitting scenes, and certified on the protocol-v2 calibration pairs.
Every 3DMatch training scene had appeared in some earlier log, so scenes that are fresh with respect to all earlier work do not exist; the fresh scenes are disjoint from every set that fits, calibrates, chooses or gates the final method.
No fresh outdoor or FCGF data remained: on the protocol-v2 sets the final method is reported as not blind.

\paragraphb{Classifier choice.}
Four kinds are candidates for the controller's two classifiers: logistic regression ($C=1$, the protocol-v2 controller, and $C=0.1$), logistic regression on degree-2 features ($C=0.1$) and gradient-boosted trees (depth 3, 300 iterations, learning rate 0.05).
For each dataset, estimator and ordering, choose-then-certify (\mainref{sec:certification}{Sec.~3.4}) runs once per kind on the fitting pairs, and the kind whose chosen candidate has the lowest out-of-fold cost is certified; the calibration pairs play no part in the choice.
Chosen kinds (compatibility order, \SCP\,/\,TurboReg): fresh scenes \val{v3.sc.v3_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}\,/\,\val{v3.tr.v3_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}; indoor FPFH \val{v3.sc.v2_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}\,/\,\val{v3.tr.v2_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}; KITTI FPFH \val{v3.sc.v2_kitti_fpfh.compat.clf}\,/\,\val{v3.tr.v2_kitti_fpfh.compat.clf}; indoor FCGF \val{v3.sc.v2_indoor_fcgf.compat.clf}\,/\,\val{v3.tr.v2_indoor_fcgf.compat.clf}; for MAC, fresh scenes \val{v3.mc.v3_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}, indoor FPFH \val{v3.mc.v2_indoor_fpfh.compat.clf}, KITTI FPFH \val{v3.mc.v2_kitti_fpfh.compat.clf} and indoor FCGF \val{v3.mc.v2_indoor_fcgf.compat.clf}; with FCGF on KITTI every certified configuration is the full budget, which needs no classifier (\cref{tab:v3full,tab:v3full2} list every configuration).

\paragraphb{Gate.}
Fourteen signals of the putative correspondences (Lowe-ratio order, cap 4{,}000), computed before any estimator run: $\log N$; the 10th and 50th percentiles of the Lowe ratio and its mean over the 250 most distinctive correspondences; the largest, 10th-largest and mean first-order degree of the 100 anchors among the 500 most distinctive correspondences (the ordering's first pass); the 1st, 10th, 50th and 250th largest second-order anchor support (\mainref{eq:score}{Eq.~2}); the number of correspondences consistent with at least 25 and 50\,\% of the anchors; and the anchors' mutual consistency.
The label of a training pair is whether \method, as certified, costs more than the full budget on it; logistic regression or boosted trees and a threshold are chosen by the 5-fold out-of-fold total cost on the fitting and calibration pairs.
A gated pair runs the full budget, whose result is the reference, so the gate can only remove losses: if $L$ is the set of pairs the certified policy loses, the gated policy loses $L$ minus the gated pairs, overall and in every difficulty group, and every certificate holds unchanged.
The gate's signals return the compatibility order as well, so a pair that is not gated reuses them; the order is identical to that of the ordering alone.

\paragraphb{Cost accounting.}
The signals cost \val{gate.signals.ms}\,ms per pair and replace the ordering's first pass (\val{gate.order.ms}\,ms alone), and one prediction costs \val{gate.pred.logit.ms}\,ms (logistic) or \val{gate.pred.hgb.ms}\,ms (trees), measured in one process with the default multi-threaded BLAS (\texttt{scripts/gate\_cost.py}).
Offline, every pair is charged one prediction, gated pairs the signals plus the full budget, and other pairs the difference between signals and ordering (compatibility order) or the signals (ratio order).
The gate was trained, and first evaluated, with an earlier accounting that charged no prediction and took the signals' cost from the feature runs, which were timed under parallel load; the evaluation was then re-run with the consistent accounting above. It reproduced every choice and every per-pair outcome and changed the offline speed-ups by at most \val{v3.acct.maxdiff.group} for the group-conditional certificates and \val{v3.acct.maxdiff.marg} for the marginal ones, in either direction.

\paragraphb{Live overhead.}
Live, on the fresh pairs that the gate lets through and on which \method with and without the gate stop at the same stage, so that the estimator does the same work, the gated method takes \val{v3.gate.live.overhead.min}--\val{v3.gate.live.overhead.max}\,ms longer per pair (median), an order of magnitude more than the measured cost of signals and prediction.
We diagnosed it on development pairs only, since the blind pairs may not be timed again: there the overhead is \val{gate.dev.overhead.min}--\val{gate.dev.overhead.max}\,ms (median, identical decisions), of which about \val{gate.dev.pred.ms}\,ms is the classifier's prediction and the rest the estimator stages running slightly slower on identical work; limiting the classifier's thread pool to one thread changed it by \val{gate.dev.thread.diff}\,ms, and computing the timing harness's untimed offline checks after all timed runs instead of between them changed neither the overhead nor the speed-ups. We could not reproduce the larger overhead of the blind run and did not establish its cause; the reported times include it as measured.
For \SCP above 30\,\% overlap it outweighs the gate's saving (\mainref{tab:blind}{Table~1}).

\paragraphb{Untimed configuration.}
With FCGF on KITTI the certified final method is the full budget itself, for all three estimators and both orderings, as is \method without the gate; its live timing was stopped as redundant for \SCP and TurboReg and, by the rule of addendum~B, not run for MAC, and the tables report 1.00$\times$ by construction.

\section{Further evidence for the final method}
\label{supp:v3evidence}

\paragraphb{Component ablation.}
\Cref{tab:v3ablation} adds the two changes of the final method one at a time, on the earlier test sets only (the fresh blind test was evaluated once, as pre-registered, for the final method and for \method without the gate): the classifier kind chosen on the fitting pairs, and the hardness gate with the logistic controller of protocol~v2; every variant is chosen and certified by the same rules.
The gate alone keeps the ladder and the certified thresholds of \method without it in every case, and on the \val{abl3.gate.n} conditions where \method cuts it is faster on \val{abl3.gate.faster} (by up to \val{abl3.gate.gain.max}\,\%, offline) and, since it only sends pairs to the full budget, never riskier.
Choosing the classifier is faster on \val{abl3.clf.faster} conditions and slower on \val{abl3.clf.slower}, mostly with FCGF, and raises the risk on \val{abl3.clf.riskier} (by at most \val{abl3.clf.dr.max}\pp, within the level); the final method, which combines both, is faster than \method without the gate on \val{abl3.v3.faster} and slower on \val{abl3.v3.slower}.

\paragraphb{Setup.}
A pre-registered additional measurement (protocol~v3, addendum~C and its amendment for MAC): nothing is chosen, fitted or certified on its results.
PyTorch ports of \SCP, TurboReg and MAC's graph stages, of the compatibility ordering, of the gate's signals and of the controller follow the CPU code operation by operation (distances in double precision, graph products in single precision); MAC's maximal-clique search stays on the CPU, as in the authors' implementations.
The certified controllers and gates are exported as arrays whose probabilities equal the fitted classifiers' (checked to $10^{-9}$), and the correspondences of the CPU live-timing samples (300 pairs per test set) are exported single-threaded, exactly as logged.
Every run is timed with the device synchronised, the order of the runs rotated from pair to pair, after untimed warm-up runs, on a \val{gpu.device} (Google Colab).

{\interlinepenalty=10000 \paragraphb{Checks.}
Before the run, the ports reproduced the CPU decisions and outcomes on fitting pairs; \SCP's local search ranks integer scores whose ties PyTorch breaks differently from NumPy, which the GPU runs keep.\par}

\paragraphb{Coverage.}\looseness=1
All three estimators are timed on all \val{gpu.sets.n} test sets of addendum~C and its amendment, \val{gpu.conds.n} conditions (with FCGF on KITTI every configuration is the full budget).

\paragraphb{GPU against CPU.}
For \SCP and TurboReg the GPU speed-up of the final method is within \val{gpu.cpugap.max} of the CPU's on every test set; MAC's, whose clique search stays on the host CPU, is larger where \method cuts most (\val{gpu.mc.th.v3.speed}\X against \val{v3.mc.th.compat.live.speed}\X on the fresh pairs above 30\,\% overlap).

\section{Limitations revisited}
\label{supp:limits}
Four questions about the protocol can be answered with the logs we already have: how many calibration pairs a certificate needs, how the guarantee holds group by group, how much the choice of $\varepsilon$ and $G$ matters, and what bounding the worst case costs. The studies that re-run the selection (calibration size, sensitivity, re-fitted controllers) use the development benchmarks only, so the blind test sets are evaluated once.

{\clubpenalty=10000 \paragraphb{How many calibration pairs a certificate needs.}\looseness=-1
With no loss observed among $n$ calibration pairs, the exact binomial test certifies a 1\,\% level at $\delta=0.1$ only if $n\ge230$, and the group-conditional certificate needs $339$ pairs in each of its three groups at level $\delta/3$.
\Cref{tab:calsize} checks this on random subsets of the calibration pools of 3DMatch and KITTI (20 subsets per size, from 100 pairs to the whole pool), re-certifying the thresholds of \method's selected configuration and evaluating each certificate once on test.
The group-conditional certificate first appears between 900 and 1{,}200 pairs, as the bound predicts ($3\times339=1{,}017$).\par}

The marginal certificate appears from 300 pairs, but on KITTI up to \val{cal.kitti.marg.over.max}\,\% of the marginal certificates (TurboReg) exceed the 1\,\% level on test, while no group-conditional certificate does at any size (largest risk \val{cal.group.worst.max}\,\%).
The safety of the group-conditional certificate is paid for with a calibration set of about a thousand pairs.

{\clubpenalty=10000 \paragraphb{Checks of the guarantee.}
Proposition~1 bounds the risk of every difficulty group, and hence of any mixture of easy and hard pairs; the worst mixture puts all its weight on the group with the largest risk.
\Cref{tab:evidence} reports this worst-case risk on test.\par}

{\clubpenalty=10000 For \method it is at most \val{ev.worst.group.fpfh.max}\,\% on the FPFH test sets and \val{ev.worst.group.max}\,\% overall; the marginal certificate, evaluated on the same groups, reaches up to \val{ev.worst.marg.max}\,\%, which a test set made only of such pairs would lose.

Of the \val{ev.groups.n} test groups, \val{ev.groups.over} exceed 1\,\% for \method (at most \val{ev.groups.over.risk}\,\%, \val{ev.groups.over.lost} of \val{ev.groups.over.n} pairs); the certificate bounds the expected risk of each group, and in a group of \val{ev.groups.over.n} pairs a single pair moves the realized risk by \val{ev.groups.over.step}\pp.
Scenes are not certified groups, and with few pairs each their risks scatter more (up to the value in the table).\par}

\paragraphb{Re-fitted controllers (protocol~v2).}\looseness=-1
Re-fitting the controller on ten bootstrap resamples of the fitting pairs and re-certifying its thresholds changes the speed-up moderately (development benchmarks); \val{ev.refit.over} of the \val{ev.refit.n} re-fitted certificates exceed 1\,\% on test (at most \val{ev.refit.risk.max}\,\%), consistent with a guarantee that holds with probability \mbox{$1-\delta=0.9$}.

\section{Equal-time comparison: details}
\label{supp:equaltime}
\paragraphb{Variants.}
\emph{Fewer seeds}: \SCP on all correspondences with a maximum seed ratio of 0.025 or 0.01 instead of 0.2 (non-maximum suppression already limits the seeds to a few hundred).
\emph{Fewer cliques}: TurboReg with $K_1=500$ or $250$ instead of 2{,}000.

{\clubpenalty=10000 \emph{Uniform sampling}: a random subset of $N$ correspondences.
\emph{FastMAC-style sampling}~\cite{zhang2024fastmac}: compatibility weights $W_{ij}=1-S_{ij}^2/(2d^2)$, kept if above 0.999, on all correspondences (second-order graph $W\odot WW$ as published, or the first-order graph $W$ as a cheaper variant); the degree signal $s=W\mathbf 1$ is high-pass filtered, $f=(\mathrm{Diag}(s)-W)s$, and $N$ correspondences are drawn without replacement with probability $\propto f_i^2$.
The signal is computed once per pair and charged to every budget.\par}

{\clubpenalty=10000 \paragraphb{Measurement.}
Recall is measured on every second test pair (by index; \val{eq.n.bh} pairs of the held-out scenes and \val{eq.n.lo} 3DLoMatch pairs; FastMAC-style second order on every fourth, fewer seeds/cliques on every eighth), and time live on \val{eq.sc.live.n} test pairs.\par}

\section{Certified adaptive pose search (CAPS)}
\label{supp:caps}
The certification machinery is not specific to correspondence budgets.
We also applied it to a pose search, which replaces a robust estimator altogether, and compare it with RANSAC.

{\clubpenalty=10000 \paragraphb{Search.}
Rotations come from a scrambled Sobol sequence~\cite{sobol1967} mapped to SO(3)~\cite{shoemake1992uniform}, in nested grids of 256 to 2{,}048 rotations.

\paragraphb{Scoring.}
For a rotation $R$, every correspondence votes for the translation $\mathbf q_i-R\mathbf p_i$ (source centred); votes are hashed into cells whose side grows with the grid's covering angle, and the densest of two staggered cell grids scores $R$.
The best basins after pose-space non-maximum suppression are refined by annealed least squares.

\paragraphb{Schedule and controller.}
The anytime schedule (256 rotations, 1 basin) $\to$ (256, 4) $\to$ (1{,}024, 4) $\to$ (2{,}048, 16) was chosen on validation; its reference is the schedule run to completion.
\emph{Progressive voting} scores rotations on growing prefixes of the ratio-sorted correspondences and keeps the best quarter in each round, successive halving~\cite{jamieson2016successive} applied to rotation search (CAPS-fast); full voting scores all of them (CAPS-accurate).
The controller is the Markov controller of \mainref{sec:controller}{Sec.~3.3}, certified with the marginal certificate; cost is counted in correspondence evaluations (CE).\par}

{\clubpenalty=10000 \paragraphb{Results.}
On the same 1{,}000 mutual FPFH correspondences and the official RMSE criterion, CAPS-fast is \val{A.psh.m.ransac.live}\X faster than RANSAC with its classical stopping rule on 3DMatch at equal recall and \val{A.psh.lo.ransac.live}\X faster on 3DLoMatch, where it registers \val{A.psh.lo.ransac.pdrr}\pp more pairs (\cref{tab:caps}); CAPS-accurate registers \val{A.full.lo.ransac.pdrr}\pp more 3DLoMatch pairs than RANSAC, but runs at \val{A.full.lo.ransac.live}--\val{A.full.m.ransac.live}\X its speed.\par}

{\clubpenalty=10000 The Markov controller is the cheapest certified family and the only one non-inferior on 3DMatch (\cref{tab:capscontrollers}).
RANSAC's classical rule spends \val{A.waste.psh.m.share}\,\% and \val{A.waste.psh.lo.share}\,\% of its budget on pairs with fewer than ten true correspondences, which it almost never registers (\cref{tab:ransacwaste}).
Against fixed search, CAPS is cheaper than the largest configuration run once on 3DMatch (\val{A.psh.m.direct.ceratio}--\val{A.full.m.direct.ceratio}\X fewer CE) but not on 3DLoMatch (\val{A.psh.lo.direct.ceratio}--\val{A.full.lo.direct.ceratio}\X).\par}

\section{Evaluation pitfalls}
\label{supp:pitfalls}
Adaptive methods are judged by modest cost ratios, so the evaluation can create or erase the result.
\Cref{tab:pitfalls} lists the pitfalls we encountered in this study, with the rule we now follow; three of them changed our own numbers.

\caption{\textbf{Evaluation pitfalls for adaptive registration}, each observed in this study.}

\toprule
Pitfall & What happened & Rule\\

Weak fixed baseline & The pose-search schedule run to completion re-votes at every grid density; against the largest configuration run once, the 3DLoMatch saving of CAPS turns from 1.5\X into \val{A.psh.lo.direct.ceratio}--\val{A.full.lo.direct.ceratio}\X. & Compare with the strongest configuration run once and with the static frontier.\\
Misattributed timing gaps & A validation-vs-test gap in full-budget time (\val{pit.full.val}\,ms vs.\ \val{pit.full.m}\,ms for \SCP) looked like machine load; it was content (validation pairs have fewer correspondences). Re-timing reproduced the logs within a few percent. & Compare like with like; time a method with its reference in the same run.\\
Micro\-bench\-marks & The ordering costs \val{abl.torder.micro}\,ms warm and alone, \val{abl.torder.log}\,ms per pair inside the parallel logs. & Charge in-pipeline costs; let live timing decide.\\
